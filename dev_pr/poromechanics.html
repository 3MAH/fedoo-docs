Poromechanics

fedoo provides a Biot-type poromechanics module for saturated porous media: a monolithic (u, PorePressure) coupling between the solid skeleton momentum balance and the fluid mass balance (Darcy flow + storage). The skeleton constitutive law is reused unchanged — it never sees the pore pressure. Any fedoo.constitutivelaw law works, including the simcoon hyperelastic laws (NEOHC, MOORI, YEOHH, ISHAH, GETHH, SWANH).

Governing equations

The total Cauchy stress follows the Biot/Terzaghi effective stress split (\(p > 0\) in compression):

\[\sigma = \sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}(\varepsilon) - \alpha \, p \, \mathbf{I}\]

and the fluid mass balance combines Darcy flow, storage and the volumetric coupling (backward-Euler in time):

\[\frac{1}{M}\dot p + \alpha\, \dot\varepsilon_v - \nabla \cdot \left( \frac{k}{\mu_f} \nabla p \right) = 0\]

where \(\alpha\) is the Biot coefficient, \(M\) the Biot modulus, \(k\) the intrinsic permeability and \(\mu_f\) the fluid viscosity. In finite strain the volumetric strain measure is the logarithmic volume change \(\varepsilon_v = \ln J\).

Weak forms and choosing a variant

Each variant is a WeakFormSum of the momentum balance, the Darcy term and the storage/coupling term. They differ by the treatment of the volume change of the skeleton.

  • fedoo.weakform.PoroMechanicsSimple (default). Unknowns u and PorePressure; the skeleton is handled by the standard StressEquilibrium. Use it unless you have a specific reason not to.

  • fedoo.weakform.PoroMechanics. Adds a third unknown, Pressure, which enforces the volume change of the skeleton (StressEquilibriumMixed). It is only useful when the skeleton itself is nearly incompressible (drained Poisson ratio close to 0.5) and the sample is confined. Its pressure oscillates near free-traction faces (Mandel consolidation, unconfined compression, indentation). bulk_modulus is required with nlgeom.

Soft tissues and gels. A saturated tissue behaves as incompressible when it is loaded quickly, but this comes from the fluid, which has no time to escape (undrained response), not from the skeleton. The fluid part is already carried by PorePressure, and the drained skeleton is compressible (\(\nu \approx 0.1\) to \(0.3\)). Use PoroMechanicsSimple with a compressible skeleton law, and a Taylor-Hood interpolation if the loading is fast (see below).

Element interpolation: equal order or Taylor-Hood

Why the choice matters. When the fluid has no time to flow (low permeability, short time step) and is nearly incompressible (large biot_modulus), the mass balance reduces to \(\alpha\,\dot\varepsilon_v \approx 0\): the skeleton cannot change volume, and the pore pressure acts as the Lagrange multiplier that enforces this constraint. This is the undrained limit. It has the same mathematical structure as incompressible elasticity or Stokes flow, a saddle-point problem, and its discretization is stable only if the pressure field is not too rich compared with the displacement field. This requirement is the inf-sup condition, also called the LBB condition after Ladyzhenskaya, Babuska and Brezzi.

When it is violated, some pressure patterns produce no nodal force on the displacement field. Nothing in the equations controls them, so they appear as spurious oscillations of the pressure, typically alternating in sign from one node to the next (checkerboard modes). They are strongest near drained boundaries and loaded faces during the first increments. In the strictly undrained limit, the system matrix is singular.

Equal-order interpolation uses the same element for u and PorePressure, e.g. hex8 for both. It does not satisfy the inf-sup condition, but the storage term (\(1/M\)) and the Darcy term stabilise it as long as the fluid can flow within a time step. A classical rule of thumb for linear elements is that oscillations stay small when

\[\Delta t \gtrsim \frac{h^2}{6\,c_v}, \qquad c_v = \frac{k}{\mu_f}\Big(K + \tfrac43 G\Big),\]

with \(h\) the element size near the drained boundary or the load, \(c_v\) the consolidation coefficient and \(K\), \(G\) the drained bulk and shear moduli of the skeleton (\(K + \tfrac43 G\) is the oedometric modulus). This is the criterion of Vermeer and Verruijt (1981), derived for one-dimensional consolidation with linear elements and incompressible constituents; with a hydraulic conductivity \(k_h = k\,\gamma_w/\mu_f\) it reads \(\Delta t \gtrsim \gamma_w h^2/(6\,E_{oed}\,k_h)\), the form quoted in the Abaqus documentation. Fine meshes, low permeabilities and short first steps of a fast loading are therefore the risky cases. Equal-order elements are fine for most consolidation analyses that do not need the very first instants.

Taylor-Hood interpolation interpolates the displacement one order higher than the pressure: quadratic u and linear PorePressure. This pair satisfies the inf-sup condition and gives a smooth pressure even in the undrained limit, at the cost of a quadratic mesh.

How to build it in fedoo. A CombinedElement defines a new element type in which each variable may use its own interpolation. Its base element (here the quadratic one) is used for the geometry and for every variable by default; set_variable_interpolation assigns a linear interpolation to one variable, built on the vertex nodes of the quadratic element. The mesh itself stays quadratic. The combined element is registered under the name given as first argument, which must therefore be created before the assembly and passed as elm_type:

mesh = fd.mesh.box_mesh(..., elm_type="hex20")  # quadratic mesh

# New element type "hex20lbb": hex20 for u and the geometry,
# hex8 (vertex nodes only) for PorePressure.
elm = fd.lib_elements.CombinedElement("hex20lbb", "hex20")
elm.set_variable_interpolation("PorePressure", "hex8")

assembly = fd.Assembly.create(wf, mesh, elm_type="hex20lbb")

With the three-field PoroMechanics, interpolate the skeleton Pressure linearly as well (elm.set_variable_interpolation("Pressure", "hex8")).

Note

With a Taylor-Hood element, the PorePressure DOFs of the mid-side nodes carry no physical value: the pressure is defined by the vertex nodes only. A nodal plot of PorePressure that includes the mid-side nodes is therefore misleading. Read the pressure at the vertex nodes, or at the integration points with assembly.get_gp_results(pb.space.variable("PorePressure"), pb.get_dof_solution()).

Fluid properties and permeability models

Fluid-phase parameters are grouped in fedoo.constitutivelaw.PoroFluidProperties: biot_coefficient (\(\alpha\), default 1.0 — the Terzaghi limit), biot_modulus (\(M\); None means an incompressible fluid, \(1/M = 0\)), permeability, fluid_viscosity and initial_porosity. fluid_density is stored but not used yet (reserved for gravity loading).

permeability may be a constant or a callable k(J, sv) for deformation-dependent permeability. Two standard models are provided:

  • fedoo.constitutivelaw.HolmesMowPermeability — articular cartilage model, permeability drops in compression;

  • fedoo.constitutivelaw.KozenyCarmanPermeability — porosity driven, with \(\phi(J)\) from solid mass conservation.

In finite strain (nlgeom), both weak-form variants compute lnJ at gauss points and feed \(J = e^{\ln J}\) to the callable. If a callable permeability is used in a context where lnJ is unavailable, a warning is emitted and the reference value \(k(J{=}1)\) is used.

Finite strain formulation

The formulation follows the simcoon kinematic conventions: UL works in the \((\ln \mathbf{V}, \boldsymbol{\tau})\) box and TL in the \((\ln \mathbf{U}, \mathbf{T})\) box. Both use the same volumetric measure, \(\mathrm{tr}\,\ln\mathbf{U} = \mathrm{tr}\,\ln\mathbf{V} = \ln J\). The TL weak form is the exact pull-back of the UL one, so the two modes assemble the same residual and give the same solution.

Written per unit reference volume, the model is:

\[\boldsymbol{\tau} = \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}} - \alpha p J \mathbf{I} \quad \left(\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{eff}} - \alpha p J \mathbf{C}^{-1}\right)\]
\[\alpha \dot J + \frac{J}{M}\dot p - \mathrm{Div}\left( J \mathbf{F}^{-1} \frac{k(J)}{\mu_f} \mathbf{F}^{-T} \nabla_X p \right) = 0\]

which is, per unit current volume, \(\alpha\, \mathrm{div}\, \mathbf{v} + \dot p / M - \mathrm{div}(k/\mu_f\, \nabla p) = 0\). The coefficients \(\alpha\) and \(M\) are constants.

  • UL (updated Lagrangian, log_R corotational): the recommended mode with simcoon laws. The coupling \(-\alpha p\, \mathrm{tr}(\delta\varepsilon)\) and the mass balance are assembled on the current mesh, with the geometric tangent of the pore stress. The volumetric rate is the backward-Euler rate of \(\ln J\).

  • TL (total Lagrangian, Miehe logarithmic strain): the same equations on the reference mesh. The momentum coupling is \(-\alpha p J (\mathbf{C}^{-1} : \delta\mathbf{E})\). The storage and coupling terms of the mass balance carry the factor \(J\) of \(dv = J\, dV\), with \(\delta(\ln J) = \mathbf{C}^{-1} : \delta\mathbf{E}\) and \(\delta J = J \mathbf{C}^{-1} : \delta\mathbf{E}\). The mobility is pulled back to \((k/\mu_f) J \mathbf{C}^{-1}\).

In both modes the dependence of the Darcy term on the displacement (moving mesh in UL, \(J \mathbf{C}^{-1}\) in TL), the variation of the volume element \(dv\) in the UL storage and coupling terms of the mass balance, and the derivative \(d(J\mathbf{C}^{-1})/du\) of the TL momentum coupling are not linearized: the residual is exact, these tangent blocks use frozen kinematics.

Relation to Coussy’s poromechanics. In the Lagrangian finite-strain poroelasticity of Coussy (Poromechanics, 2004), the Lagrangian porosity \(\phi = J n\) obeys \(\phi - \phi_0 = \mathbf{b} : \mathbf{E} + p/N\) and the stress \(\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{eff}} - p \mathbf{b}\), with the same Biot tensor \(\mathbf{b}\) in both (Maxwell symmetry). fedoo corresponds to \(\mathbf{b} = \alpha J \mathbf{C}^{-1}\) (exact for incompressible grains with \(\alpha = 1\)), so the coupling terms coincide with Coussy. The only difference is the storage term: fedoo uses a modulus \(M\) constant per unit current volume (\(J \dot p / M\) per reference volume), Coussy a modulus \(N\) constant per unit reference volume (\(\dot p / N\)). Both coincide for incompressible constituents (\(1/M = 0\)), which is the classical biphasic model of soft tissues.

Sign conventions

The Biot tangent blocks are intentionally non-symmetric (\(K_{up} = -\alpha\), \(K_{pu} = +\alpha/\Delta t\)), so the momentum weak forms set assembly_options["assume_sym"] = False. Do not re-enable the symmetric assumption: it would mirror the one-sided coupling into a phantom block and split the assembly, breaking the Newton tangent. Pore pressure is positive in compression.

Validation

  • Terzaghi 1D consolidation vs the analytical series (< 1.5 %) — tests/test_poromech_terzaghi_analytical.py;

  • Mandel 2D vs the Abousleiman analytical solution, including the Mandel-Cryer non-monotonic pressure peak (~1 %) — tests/test_poromech_mandel_analytical.py;

  • Taylor-Hood LBB stability — tests/test_poromech_taylor_hood.py;

  • Finite strain with a simcoon Neo-Hookean skeleton (NEOHC, UL) and Holmes-Mow permeability: exact confined kinematics \(\ln J = \ln(1 + \delta/L)\) at the drained state — tests/test_poromech_finite_strain.py;

  • TL against UL on the same consolidation, and the TL mass-balance tangent against finite differences — tests/test_poromech_finite_strain_tl.py;

  • 2Daxi: homogeneous undrained unconfined compression of a cylinder vs the closed-form solution (both formulations), drained relaxation, and steady radial flow through a hollow cylinder vs the logarithmic pressure profile — tests/test_poromech_axi.py. The draining case was also compared once with a 3D model of the full cylinder, with close agreement (this comparison is not part of the test suite).

Limitations

  • Large compression with simcoon hyperelastic laws (UL): beyond roughly 12 % compression the Newton loop can develop a slowly-amplifying u-p oscillation. The poro coupling blocks were verified consistent against a finite-difference Jacobian; the amplification originates in the fedoo core UL displacement tangent of hyperelastic (_Lt_from_F) laws, which is self-consistent only in the modes excited by pure mechanical loading — the Biot coupling seeds the other modes at every iteration. Workarounds: smaller total strain, an elasto-plastic/corotational law (ELISO etc., which are unaffected), or finer load steps do not help (the threshold is on the strain level, not the step size).

  • 2Daxi is supported in small strain and in UL, like StressEquilibrium. It is not available in TL.

  • The permeability tensor is isotropic (\(k \mathbf{I}\)); anisotropic permeability is not implemented.

  • No gravity/body-force term for the fluid yet (fluid_density is reserved).
